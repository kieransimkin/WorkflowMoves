# Storyboard lyric videos and Shorts

Turn the song's meaning, sound and campaign identity into a visual plan and an asset pack that a renderer or editor can use. Own finding, downloading or generating the required assets, with evidence of their origin and actual readiness. Choose the storytelling approach for this song and audience rather than applying one house formula to every release. A storyboard or baseline concept is not evidence of a finished video or audience success.

Every run ends with the retrospective in section 8, including an explicit search for improvements to this skill. Record `Blogworthiness: PASS` or `Blogworthiness: FAIL`, reason and source evidence in the existing report; a PASS also needs its angle, duplicate check and delivery next action under the project's completion workflow.

## 1. Establish the release and the brief

Start at the named release folder and its current report or manifest. Read the owning project's instructions, the campaign plan's keyword, visual-style and outreach sections, accepted creative notes, canonical lyrics, master identity, cue/LRC/word timing, current visual-language analysis, approved artwork/video and prior storyboard decisions. Inspect only relevant campaign records and their linked analysis; do not crawl releases, private histories or media caches.

Confirm artist, song, campaign stage, audience, desired listening action, full-song versus excerpt scope, destination surfaces, duration, aspect ratio, resources and any treatment already requested. Keep artist catalogues and private evidence separate. Read the project's voice guidance before writing editorial copy; keep that copy distinct from sung lyrics. Normal storyboarding includes the local asset sourcing, detailed prompt packs and built-in GPT baseline drafts required below. Preserve existing cost limits; paid external generation, new licence/account commitments, model-weight downloads, uploads, schedules and public saves retain their own authority.

Create a short evidence map with source paths/IDs, hashes for selected media and timing, dates and authority: approved/canonical, observed, analysis hypothesis, proposed or unavailable. Conflicting masters or lyrics need resolution before exact timing; missing analytics need not prevent a provisional creative plan. Reuse saved evidence first. If this is a campaign-plan revision, preserve its required sections rather than replacing it with a storyboard.

## 2. Derive the creative constraints from the evidence

- **Lyrics:** map performed order, speaker/point of view, events, emotional turns, imagery, repeated lines and what changes when they recur. Distinguish a literal event from an inferred metaphor. Preserve ambiguity and canonical wording; do not invent an artist's life story from a lyric.
- **Audio:** use source-bound StemLab results and existing listening notes to identify phrase boundaries, tempo/meter confidence, energy/timbre changes, vocal density, tension, releases, drops and instrumental space. Attach each proposed visual beat to an actual lyric or musical event. A detector score is not attended listening or an approved beat grid.
- **Visual identity:** inspect the actual approved cover, representative campaign assets and visual analysis for palette roles, motifs, typography, texture, lighting, framing and motion. State the invariants, permitted variation and purposeful departures. Use PrismMoves/PixelCue for needed measurements and visual-identity assessment for collection conclusions; avoid rerunning expensive analysis without a concrete gap.
- **Audience:** inspect relevant existing first-party analytics and earlier tests, then current primary platform guidance and a small, varied set of comparable examples when useful. Follow [evidence and learning](references/evidence-and-learning.md) to connect a finding to a specific decision, with its limits.

Where available, use the maintained analysis, loop-selection and lyric-timing skills. Read current DanceFlow capability and release guidance before a new tool invocation. StemLab owns audio/timing evidence, FrameRudiments offline composition, DanceRudiments native movement, and DanceMoves live web motion. Other available tools are conditional on a real need; no new engine or model run is required for a written plan.

Before locking a treatment or asset list, follow [current native capabilities and analysis planning](references/tool-capability-planning.md). Check the latest official FrameRudiments and StemLab releases against the exact selected runtimes, read their relevant tagged feature/API documentation and maintained workflows, and record the check date and source identities. A copied guide's old version, an MCP tool list, a release-note milestone or a familiar effect name is not a complete current capability review. Refresh at a material handoff or when the selected runtime changes; preserve a frozen render's source/version pins.

Use the current FrameRudiments renderer/effect inventory to inform creative alternatives: native movement and sprites/3D, rich lyrics, masks/mattes/keying, transitions, styles/warps/procedural fields, feedback/temporal effects and applicable newer authoring, video, interchange or control routes. Use StemLab's retained rhythm/structure, word/line timing, phrase/loop, Fourier, native-motion and other relevant analysis through their maintained workflows. Consider what serves this song and explicitly record relevant unavailable or unneeded routes. Do not invent extra effects, analysis, downloads or generation just to exercise features.

For each chosen effect or audio-driven scene, link the exact feature/configuration route to its StemLab evidence, original source time, input/layer contract, backend and required assets. Separate documented, installed, host-qualified and actually previewed states. A capability found only in a newer release remains conditional until its required runtime is available; a generic raster baseline cannot prove a native effect works. Save this mapping in the storyboard's capability/analysis plan and carry it into the generation briefs and production handoff.

For excerpts, use `find-loopable-song-sections` or its maintained workflow to select a musically and narratively complete passage. Preserve original source bounds; do not choose an arbitrary duration and cut a word, setup or punchline to fit it. A non-looping mini-story is a valid Short. A full lyric video should develop across the song; a Short needs an intelligible local promise and payoff rather than a scaled-down full-film plot.

## 3. Explore genuinely different storytelling treatments

Unless the user has already fixed the treatment, sketch at least these two approaches before choosing. A hybrid is useful when it resolves a real weakness. Make the alternatives visually concrete; changing a preset or colour is not a different story.

| Approach | What to design | When it can fit; what to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lyric-led narrative | Follow the lyric's events or emotional causality step by step: situation, development, turn and earned ending. Carry characters, props and spatial continuity between scenes. | Eventful lyrics, changing viewpoints or a clear emotional journey. Avoid illustrating every noun, inventing literal facts or forcing a three-act plot onto ambiguous writing. |
| Single visual or effect hook | One memorable image, action, transformation or physical rule appears early and develops through variation, escalation or a reveal tied to the music. Specify what changes, when and why the viewer stays. | A refrain, one emotional idea, an instrumental moment or a compact Short. One hook can sustain the video without frequent cuts, but an unchanging wallpaper effect needs a better reason to hold attention. |
| Hybrid or another justified form | A recurring hook anchors lyric-led developments; alternatively consider performance, kinetic typography, a visual metaphor, a process/reveal or a contemplative tableau. | Use the form that expresses this song. Explain any nonliteral mapping and protect lyric clarity. Do not add visual noise merely to increase event count. |

For each candidate, describe its first decoded frame, initial visual promise, development or deliberate sustained state, any turn/payoff, ending or loop, lyric/music fit, campaign fit, production route and audience hypothesis. A contemplative treatment can hold one tableau while the lyric or musical tension develops; explain its reason to continue without manufacturing a climax. State which evidence supports it and what remains a creative bet. Avoid generic claims such as "cinematic visuals" or "viral hook" without an actual subject, action and timing.

Compare candidates qualitatively for meaning, immediate comprehension at phone size, reason to continue, musical responsiveness, legibility, distinctive identity, feasibility and evidence strength. Select one with reasons and retain the strongest alternative as a falsifiable test where practical. Honour a user-selected approach; inspect its weaknesses without reopening an unnecessary competition.

## 4. Build the timed storyboard

Use [the storyboard template](assets/storyboard-template.md), adapting detail to the job. Put the result and learning record in the owning campaign's existing dated report/evidence structure; do not overwrite accepted work.

For every shot or visual state specify:

- source in/out and output in/out, exact lyric line or explicit instrumental state, audio/cue trigger and timing authority; keep source seconds/samples separate from output frames, and label estimated timings;
- composition, focal subject, action, camera/motion, transition and the visual information gained or feeling developed; specify effect onset, growth, release and reset instead of naming an effect alone;
- viewer promise, progression and payoff; map planned attention events to timestamps so later retention can be compared with actual scenes;
- displayed sung text versus separate editorial text, line holds/clears, type treatment and reserved quiet space; do not make an unsung word appear as a performed lyric;
- asset IDs/provenance, continuity/seed or reference requirements, foreground/background/effect order, exact current native feature/configuration route, linked StemLab evidence and any unverified capability;
- destination crop/overlay constraints, key pose and readability checks, ending behaviour and dependencies still awaiting review.

For a full video cover all lyric and instrumental intervals, including deliberate holds and gaps. For a Short, cover its whole selected output and any intended repeat or ending. A nonlinear visual edit must not silently reorder the canonical soundtrack or sung words. Do not repeat a chorus scene unchanged unless that recurrence serves the chosen story.

Plan destination safe regions before image prompts or layout, using current native evidence and the project's platform-safe-region workflow; fixed rectangles are provisional aids. Give each destination a variant when one composition cannot fit. Follow local cover/title/artist requirements and plan a compositionally distinct cover for each distinct video. Keep essential subjects and glyphs clear through camera/effect extremes, not just in a clean source plate. Preserve readable text, controllable playback and sensible motion/flash behaviour. Target at least 60 fps for authored motion and previews; an encoded rate or duplicated frames do not prove motion cadence.

## 5. Find and prepare the actual assets

Read [asset sourcing and generation](references/asset-preparation.md) and use [the per-asset prompt template](assets/asset-prompt-template.md). Turn the selected board into a deduplicated asset register linked to shot IDs. Cover scene backgrounds/clean plates, separate foreground objects and sprites, textures/mattes, footage, 3D props/environments, rigged characters and other mixed media that the board actually needs. Choose reuse, open-licence sourcing, generation or a justified combination for each; do not leave the asset search as an unspecified production task.

Inspect suitable release assets first, then search bounded relevant open-licence sources and download the chosen files with their author, original page/file URL, exact licence, attribution and source hash. Provide the actual reference image files, not only search links. A release-cover crop needs its parent hash, crop coordinates/dimensions and a note saying what to borrow and what to exclude. Every reference needs a stable ID, visual role, permitted use, attachment order and per-asset usage notes. Website preview renders and logos can have different rights from an asset download.

For **each required generated asset and necessary distinct variant**, save a detailed shared visual specification and three complete prompts **before generating**: a Seedance prompt, a Nano Banana prompt and the exact GPT built-in baseline prompt. Define subject/action or pose, composition/camera, palette/materials/lighting, continuity, references and their usage, output/layer requirements, reserved lyric space and exclusions. Seedance is a video model family: give it the same asset/keyframe design plus a separate motion/camera plan for later video use. Do not silently substitute Seedream, claim Seedance emitted a PNG, or execute Seedance/Nano Banana jobs merely because their prompts are prepared.

Then invoke GPT's built-in image generation separately for every required generated asset/variant, using the saved still-image brief and the actual approved references. Follow the `imagegen` skill; inspect local reference images first and attach them through the supported image tool arguments. Save and inspect every baseline draft in the owning campaign, verify its dimensions/hash and alpha where requested, and link it to the asset, prompt and reference hashes. A contact sheet, moodboard, unexecuted prompt or earlier unrelated image does not replace these individual baselines. If access, limits or a required reference block generation, keep only the affected assets pending with exact reasons; do not report the pack complete.

For 3D work, source or create the real compatible mesh, materials and, when needed, skeleton, skin weights and animation clips through a verified route. A GPT character turnaround is a concept baseline, not a rigged character model. Retain both the concept and the separate production dependency until the actual files import and the required poses/animation work in the intended consumer. Do not assume that an OBJ, a marketplace thumbnail or a synthetic render contains a rig.

## 6. Make the plan visually reviewable

Create a small contact sheet with the opening, meaningful development and payoff for each serious contender, then build the selected board's panels from the prepared assets and individual GPT baselines. Early sketches can compare concepts, but do not satisfy the selected board's asset-generation requirement. Provide source captions, reference/asset IDs and useful alt text. A generated concept is an illustration, never proof of a rendered effect, rights clearance or audience response.

Use a bounded animatic only when timing or a dynamic hook cannot be assessed from panels and its cost is in scope. Route it through FrameRudiments, preserve the source clock and target at least 60 fps. Test the earliest visible state, the intended change, lyric readability at phone size and the ending/seam. Seek or replay feedback effects according to their actual temporal requirements. Keep normal-speed visual review, technical checks and attended listening distinct.

Compare the alternatives against the same brief, excerpt and constraints where possible. A private preference review tests comprehension and fit; it does not establish audience retention. Record what was actually seen/heard, by whom when relevant, and what the comparison changed. If an optional medium such as an animatic cannot usefully be produced in scope, retain the written/diagrammed board and name the omission. Required GPT baselines remain explicit pending items until produced or resolved under the asset-preparation contract.

## 7. Hand off for production and later learning

Deliver the selected board, alternatives/rejection reasons, source map, contact sheet, asset register and acquired files, complete Seedance/Nano Banana/GPT prompt packs, actual reference images with usage notes, every required GPT baseline draft, implementation/timing notes, acceptance checks and unresolved production dependencies. Link each shot to its assets and each generated asset to its prompt/reference/output evidence. State `proposed`, `baseline_generated`, `production_ready`, `previewed` or `accepted` accurately. A baseline-complete pack can still have a named mesh/rig or video-generation dependency; do not conflate the two. Rendering and publishing retain their existing workflow and approval requirements.

Before an authorised audience test, write a prediction, the changed variable, platform-specific primary capture metric, retention/meaning guardrails, comparison baseline, observation window and sufficient-exposure rule. See [evidence and learning](references/evidence-and-learning.md). Capture, sustained attention and downstream listening are different outcomes. Record the next review point and owner; do not create a schedule or publish test variants merely because a test plan exists.

## 8. Introspect and improve this skill on every run

Complete a short retrospective even for an aborted, blocked or analytics-free run. Record the skill revision/hash, inputs, selected approach, observed review results, uncertainty, avoidable friction and what should change in this skill itself. Later audience results supplement this record; they do not replace the immediate process review.

Ask whether intake missed useful release evidence, the capability review was current, a suitable native effect or saved analysis was overlooked, alternatives were genuinely different, the hook paid off, lyric meaning and identity survived, timing/safe regions were usable, evidence was overstated, asset searches or references were useful, prompts preserved identity across providers, all required baselines were actually made, 3D readiness was overstated, the production handoff was actionable, or a step wasted effort. Identify the smallest reusable correction supported by what actually happened.

Apply a worthwhile, authorised correction to the maintained canonical skill/reference/template, then validate it and replay the relevant case plus a contrasting case. Keep public guidance free of private campaign records; store the evidence and change receipt locally. Reconcile source and installed copies through the maintained installer and normal release process rather than silently editing a managed copy. Preserve other owners' changes and publication/cost gates. If no correction is justified, record the reason. If a useful patch cannot be applied, retain the exact proposal, blocker and named continuation. Do not manufacture a skill edit after every run or promote one noisy result into a universal rule.

## Potential problems

- **Missing or conflicting lyrics, master or timing.** Keep a conceptual treatment provisional, identify the exact source conflict and withhold precise sung-word/cue claims until resolved. Do not fill the gap with a default BPM or invented words.
- **No private retention data, or unmatched public examples.** Continue with source-labelled hypotheses and a future measurement plan. A public view total or model score does not validate a storytelling mechanism.
- **A hook is striking but has no development.** Show its changed state and earned ending on the board. If a still tableau is intentional, explain its lyric/emotional function and test that specific treatment rather than forcing cuts.
- **Panels look clear but motion hides the words.** Check effect order, crop and motion extremes with the intended text and native overlays; revise before full production. A static concept does not pass dynamic readability.
- **A shared file is protected, modified or owned elsewhere.** Inspect the saved destination after a failed write, use the approved workspace editor/installer route, and retain a verified candidate plus exact installation hold if needed. Never infer installation from the source file alone.
- **Prompts exist but assets do not.** Check each generated-asset row against its saved GPT output/hash, actual references and review. Resume only missing items; a sheet of thumbnails does not close individual baseline obligations.
- **A character image is labelled rigged.** Keep the concept and 3D file states separate; require actual skeleton/skin/pose or clip playback evidence before marking a rig usable.
- **A feature guide or milestone label is stale.** Reconcile the official release, matching tagged source/API docs and selected runtime; record host qualification separately. Keep genuinely unavailable features conditional and choose a verified alternative without silently upgrading a live render.
